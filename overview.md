# Formalising "Concurrency, Causality and Conflict via Independence in Reversible Calculi"

This document provides an overview of the source code accompanying the paper 
*"Concurrency, Causality and Conflict via Independence in Reversible Calculi"*
by C. Aubert, G. Cecilia, I. Phillips and I. Ulidowski. The formalisation builds
upon the artifact for the paper [*"A Formalization of the Reversible Concurrent
Calculus CCSK<sup>P</sup> in Beluga"*](https://doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.425.5)
([Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16179366)),
refining the existing development and substantially extending it with new results.

The [**Description of the Encoding**](#description-of-the-encoding) section discusses the main formalisation
choices in detail and highlights the differences between the paper and the code.
The [**Paper-to-Artifact Table**](#paper-to-artifact-table) then concisely lists the definitions, proofs and
examples from the paper and links them to their counterparts in the formalisation.

### 4: CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### 4.1: Definition of CCSK<sup>P</sup>

##### Definition 4.1: (Co)-names, labels and keys

[Names](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L3-L5) are encoded as a type without constructors; this type is dynamically inhabited through the use of [contexts](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L30-L31) and contextual variables. This is standard for Beluga formalisations of concurrent calculi where restriction is treated as a binding operator (see, e.g., [*"Mechanizing Session-Types using a Structural View: Enforcing Linearity without Linearity"*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3622810) or [*"A Beluga Formalization of the Harmony Lemma in the π-Calculus"*](https://doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.404.1)).

Conversely, [keys](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L7-L11) are encoded by an explicit type isomorphic to the natural numbers, equipped with a decidable [inequality](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L58-L62) predicate (`neq`) at the LF level. This choice simplifies the encodings of keyed labels, proof labels and keyed prefixes by avoiding meta-level functions indexed by keys, while preserving infinite branching over keys in forward transition rules.

The encodings of [labels](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L13-L18) and [keyed labels](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/definitions.bel#L5-L8) are as expected.

Observe also that, since we lack polymorphism, equalities for different types need to be defined via a separate predicate for each type (e.g., [equality of keys](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L37-L40)).

##### Definition 4.3: Processes

In the paper, processes are not considered modulo α-equivalence, i.e., processes that differ only in the choice of names bound by restrictions are not identified. This is traditional for pen-and-paper presentations of CCS and its reversible extensions, starting from [*"A Calculus of Communicating Systems"*](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-10235-3), where the syntax of processes is not quotiented by α-equivalence.

In the encoding, conversely, [processes](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L20-L28) are considered modulo α-renaming and restriction is considered a binding operation; this choice is standard for formalisations of concurrent calculi such as the π-calculus (see, e.g., [*"π-calculus in (Co)Inductive Type Theory"*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00095-5) or [*"Proof Search Specifications of Bisimulation and Modal Logics for the π-calculus"*](https://doi.org/10.1145/1656242.1656248)). Names bound by restrictions are represented as arguments of meta-level functions, following the higher-order abstract syntax (HOAS) approach; this allows α-renaming and capture-avoiding substitutions to be delegated to the meta-level.

The set of keys of a process *keys(X)* is not encoded explicitly, as it would require additional machinery for emptiness and (non-)membership. Instead, we provide two type families encoding [standard processes (`std`)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L64-L71) and [non-occurrence of a key in a process (`notin`)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/definitions.bel#L73-L81), both defined by induction on the structure of the given process. Note that, to define the latter, we make use of the decidable inequality predicate for keys `neq` mentioned above.

##### Definition 4.5: Proof labels

As detailed in Beluga's Corner 4.10, identifying processes up to α-equivalence also requires to identify *some* proof labels by α-equivalence: this leads to the encoding of closed proof labels and open proof labels, which constitutes another difference between the paper development and the formalisation.

[Closed proof labels (`pr_lab_cl`)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L17-L25) represent proof labels without any name identification (i.e., as in Definition 4.5). Their encoding is by design over-expressive, allowing any two proof labels to be paired regardless of their key or label. This allows us to handle a simpler definition and a more direct induction principle. Well-formedness is recovered through a separate [validity](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L80-L91) predicate, which rules out spurious proof labels; this predicate will be explicitly added to the statements of lemmas and theorems whenever required (see, e.g., the statement of [Proposition 5.6 (second implication)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/connectedness-relationship-two.bel#L606-L606)).

[Open proof labels (`pr_lab_op`)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L27-L30) represent proof labels up to α-renaming of a name occurring in them. They are encoded as meta-level functions from names to closed proof labels. We extend the [validity](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L93-L97) predicate to open proof labels, to capture exactly those that are used as labels of LTS transitions: an open proof label *\a.θ* is valid if it is the α-equivalence class of a valid closed proof label *θ*, the identified name *a* occurs in *θ*, and *a* does not occur in its label *lab(θ)*. The label function `lab` and occurrence predicate `occurs` are defined below.

In the encoding, we also denote by [proof labels (`pr_lab`)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L32-L36) the union of closed and open proof labels. They also have their own [validity](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L99-L103) predicate.

*Remark*: the notation used in the artifact differs slightly from that of the paper. Specifically, "proof labels" in the paper correspond to "closed proof labels" in the artifact, while "proof labels" in the artifact denote the union of closed and open proof labels.

##### Definition 4.7: Label and key functions

The encoding of the [label and key of a closed proof label](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L38-L56) is by induction on the structure of the closed proof label. For some results, it is useful to extend the notion of key to proof labels ([key of a proof label](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L58-L62)). Note that the label of an (open) proof label is in general undefined.

Since the label and key of a proof label are defined as relations, it is useful to prove that they are [functional and total](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique.bel#L6-L66).

##### Definition 4.9: LTS for CCSK<sup>P</sup>

As explained in the [Definition 4.3 subsection](#definition-43-processes), emptiness of *keys(X)* is encoded via `std`, while non-membership of a key in *keys(X)* is encoded via `notin`.

The encoding distinguishes between closed and open transition rules, depending on whether the used proof label is closed or open. Thus, we have [closed forward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L111-L129), [open forward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L131-L148), [closed backward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L156-L174) and [open backward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L176-L194) transitions. They can be paired together by direction or by label, with the four combinations of [forward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L150-L154), [backward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L196-L200), [closed](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L202-L206) and [open](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L208-L212) transitions. [Combined](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L214-L218) transitions are encoded as the union of closed and open transitions.

For restrictions (and, without loss of generality, in the forward case), the [`fc_nu`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L128-L128) rule encodes only the transitions where the restricted name *a* does not occur in the used closed proof label *θ*; non-occurrence is enforced by having *θ* not depend on *a* in the premise of the rule. The case where *a* occurs in *θ* is recovered by the [`fo_nu_cl`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L133-L135) rule, where we require *a* to occur in *θ* but not in the label of *θ*; the presence of the auxiliary type [`occurs`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L64-L73), that enforces the occurrence of *a* in *θ*, is necessary to avoid capturing the transitions already encoded via `fc_nu`, thus ensuring that the transition between two given processes (if it exists) is unique. Finally, the [`fo_nu_op`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L146-L147) rule propagates open transitions in the case of nested restrictions.

Note that the [original formalisation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16179366) does not distinguish between closed and open proof labels or transitions, and the transition rule for restrictions prevents the restricted name *a* from occurring in *θ*; the encoding is incomplete, since the transitions where *a* occurs in *θ* are not captured.

##### Remark 4.12: Paths, connected transitions

[Paths](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L220-L225) are formalised via the reflexive and transitive closure of the combined transition relation (`step`). [Connected transitions](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L232-L235) are encoded according to their informal definition.

##### Definition 4.14: Reachable processes

[Reachable processes](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L227-L230) are encoded according to their informal definition, using the predicate for standard processes `std` instead of the set of keys of a process *keys(X)*. Similarly to the validity predicate, the hypothesis of reachability will be explicitly added to the statements of lemmas and theorems whenever required (see, e.g., the statement of [Proposition 5.6 (first implication)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/connectedness-relationship-one.bel#L10-L11)).

##### Lemma 4.15: [Loop lemma](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/basic-properties.bel#L33-L109)

The two implications of the loop lemma are proved separately, and further split between closed and open transitions.

The [`basic-properties.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/basic-properties.bel) file where the proof is located additionally contains a couple of lemmas relating the `std` and `notin` predicates, as well as a generalisation of the loop lemma to paths. 

##### Lemma 4.17: [Unique derivation trees of CCSK<sup>P</sup> transitions](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique-step.bel#L562-L588)

We actually prove a slightly stronger result than in the paper: given two processes, if there is one transition between them, the transition and its derivation tree are unique. The proof addresses closed and open transitions, as well as forward and backward transitions, separately, and proceeds by induction on the length of the derivation tree of the given transition. This lemma and most of the auxiliary results required to prove it are in the [`ccskp/unique-step.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique-step.bel) file; for instance, the file includes an [auxiliary lemma](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique-step.bel#L276-L336) showing that, for any given source and target processes, there cannot simultaneously exist both a closed and an open transition between them. The proof relies also on the uniqueness of the derivation trees of the predicates used in the LTS rules ([`neq`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/unique.bel#L31-L41), [`std`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/unique.bel#L48-L63), [`notin`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/shared/unique.bel#L70-L88), [`lab`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique.bel#L68-L82), [`key`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique.bel#L84-L99) and [`occurs`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/unique.bel#L101-L117)).

#### 4.2: Bijection between CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK

*Remark:* the encoding makes use of two modules, namely [`ccskp`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L3-L3) and [`ccsk`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/definitions.bel#L3-L3), that enclose the basic definitions for the respective calculi; this allows the same name to be reused for corresponding types (e.g., `fstep`, `bstep` or `step`). Outside of the modules, one can write `ccskp.step` and `ccsk.step` to refer to the type `step` in the `ccskp` and `ccsk` module respectively.

Throughout the rest of the development, we choose to open the `ccskp` module, meaning that every unqualified reference such as `step` is interpreted as `ccskp.step`, while CCSK definitions must be prefixed by their module name.

Some definitions and lemmas regarding CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK could not be enclosed in the modules (e.g., the type `same_direction`). For these, we adopt the convention of using the unqualified name for CCSK<sup>P</sup> (e.g., [`same_direction`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L155-L155)) and prefixing the corresponding CCSK name with `ccsk_` (e.g., [`ccsk_same_direction`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L49-L49)).

##### Definition 4.19: LTS for CCSK

As in CCSK<sup>P</sup>, we use the types `std` and `notin` instead of *keys(X)*. Unlike CCSK<sup>P</sup>, no transition needs to use α-equivalence classes of keyed labels (all the problematic cases in CCSK<sup>P</sup>, involving a synchronisation within a restriction, now simply use the keyed label *τ[k]*, that is invariant under α-renaming of the processes involved), and thus no distinction between closed and open rules is needed. [Forward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/definitions.bel#L10-L29), [backward](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/definitions.bel#L31-L50) and [combined](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L214-L218) LTS rules are encoded as expected.

Additionally, we define [paths](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/definitions.bel#L58-L63) and [connected transitions](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/definitions.bel#L65-L68) for CCSK; similarly, we prove the [loop lemma](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/basic-properties.bel#L5-L37) for CCSK.

##### Lemma 4.20: [Unique derivation trees of CCSK transitions](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/unique-step.bel#L205-L217)

As in CCSK<sup>P</sup>, the proof addresses forward and backward transitions separately, and the `code/ccsk/unique-step.bel` file contains all the auxiliary lemmas required.

##### Definition 4.21: Mappings between CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK

We define the [proof forgetful mapping](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/definitions.bel#L53-L59) and the [proof enrichment mapping](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/definitions.bel#L61-L67) as relations. We use four separate type families, that express the corresponding definition in the four closed/open and forward/backward combinations. We do not build those type families inductively, by case analysis on the given transition; instead, as in Definition 4.21, these type families just relate two transitions that satisfy the required typing conditions.

One then needs to show that these relations are actually functions, i.e., that they are functional and total. The proof of totality is particularly important in this context since it shows how the two mappings are actually constructed, by induction on the structure of the transition in the domain. The proofs, respectively contained in the [`bijection/functionality.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/functionality.bel) and [`bijection/totality.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/totality.bel) files, are split between the closed/open and forward/backward combinations, and make use of the auxiliary lemmas in the [`ccskp/lemmas-bijection.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/lemmas-bijection.bel) file. One delicate aspect to consider when building the mappings is the fact that, on the CCSK<sup>P</sup> side, we have the union of two disjoint types `fstep_cl` and `fstep_op`, whereas on the CCSK side we have only one type `ccsk.fstep`: in particular, in the case of restrictions for the proof enrichment mapping, we make use of the decidability of the predicate `occurs` to determine whether a given CCSK transition needs to be mapped to a closed or an open CCSK<sup>P</sup> transition.

##### Lemma 4.24: [Bijection between CCSK and CCSK<sup>P</sup>](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/bijection.bel)

The proof, again split between the closed/open and forward/backward combinations, follows immediately from the application of the stronger versions of Lemmas 4.17 and 4.20 (uniqueness of the derivations of CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK transitions) and their auxiliary lemmas.

### 5: Complementarity of Independence and Dependence for CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK

##### Definition 5.1: Relations on proof labels

The encodings of the [relations on closed proof labels](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/definitions.bel#L239-L321) follow their informal counterparts in Figures 13 and 14. We also extend the definitions to (open) proof labels, by requiring the corresponding judgment to hold for every possible instantiation of the bound name.

##### Remark 5.4:

The results are proved both for closed proof labels and for proof labels.

##### Proposition 5.6: Correspondence between connectedness of transitions and connectedness of proof labels

The two implications of this proposition are proved separately. Both implications make use of a number of auxiliary lemmas, that can be found in the [`ccskp/lemmas-connectedness-one.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/lemmas-connectedness-one.bel) and [`ccskp/lemmas-connectedness-two.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/lemmas-connectedness-two.bel) files respectively. These include: lemmas to infer the structure of the target process of a path, known its source; lemmas that, given a path between two processes, allow us to build a path between their subprocesses; lemmas showing that each valid proof label *θ* is realised (i.e., there exists a transition using *θ* as proof label and whose source is standard process); lemmas that, given a path, use it to build new paths.

The proof of [Proposition 5.6 (first implication)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/connectedness-relationship-one.bel) is long and tedious, but mostly follows the original informal argument in [*"Dependence and Independence for Reversible Process Calculi"*](https://doi.org/10.48550/ARXIV.2410.14699). It can be proved for both closed and open transitions/proof labels.

The proof of [Proposition 5.6 (second implication)](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/connectedness-relationship-two.bel) diverges significantly from the original informal argument, that leveraged a short proposition incorrectly; moreover, it can only be proved for closed transitions/proof labels, being false for open transitions/proof labels (see Beluga's Corner 5.7 or the comments in the [`ccskp/connectedness-relationship-two.bel` file](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/connectedness-relationship-two.bel) for more details). In short, we first prove a base case of the proposition, where additional information can be inferred; thanks to this additional information, we can then prove a stronger version of the proposition, from which the original statement immediately follows.

The proof is adapted from the one in the [original formalisation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16179366) and the proof strategy is described in detail in the corresponding paper [*"A Formalization of the Reversible Concurrent Calculus CCSK<sup>P</sup> in Beluga"*](https://doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.425.5).

### 6: Applying the Axiomatic Approach to CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK

The structure of both the [`ccskp/axioms/`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms) and [`ccsk/axioms/`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms) folders is the following:

#### 6.1: Mechanised Proofs of Axioms for CCSK<sup>P</sup>

##### Proposition 6.1: The LTS of CCSK<sup>P</sup> is pre-reversible

The additional definitions include coercions from forward and backward transitions to combined transitions ([`forw_to_comb`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L6-L10) and [`back_to_comb`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L35-L39)), the inverse of a combined transition ([`inv_tr`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L110-L113)), and same and opposite direction of transitions ([`same_direction`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L154-L158) and [`opp_direction`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L160-L164)). The auxiliary properties proved include, e.g., the proof that [backward transitions cannot use keys not present in their source](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L269-L292).

The proof of [SP](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/sp.bel#L1030-L1092) follows the original argument in [*"The Correctness of Concurrencies in (Reversible) Concurrent Calculi"*](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.JLAMP.2023.100924) and uses three auxiliary lemmas proved by induction on the structure of the given transitions. Each lemma, that has two transitions in the hypotheses, is split into four lemmas, according to the closed/open combinations of the two transitions. For this reason the proof is long and tedious, but rather straightforward.

Note that the encoding explicitly requires the pairs of transitions at opposite sides of the square to have the same direction: this may look redundant w.r.t. the informal statement of SP, but it is not. SP is usually stated in the axiomatic setting, where a forward transition and its inverse use syntactically different labels; thus, equality of labels implicitly implies same direction. In CCSK<sup>P</sup> and CCSK, instead, a forward transition and its inverse use the same label; thus, the pen-and-paper statement of SP for such calculi needs to explicit require equality of directions, in addition to labels.

For [BTI](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/bti.bel#L341-L350), we first prove that [two backward transitions are either equal or independent](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/bti.bel#L314-L332), from which the statement of BTI follows. Similarly to SP, the first result is split between the various closed/open combinations.

For [WF](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/wf.bel#L92-L106): recall that a binary relation *<* on a set *X* is well-founded if it contains no infinite descending chains  *... < x_{n+1} < x_n < ... < x_1 < x_0*. Constructively, well-foundedness is typically defined via the inductive predicate of accessibility: an element *x* of *X* is accessible if every element *x' < x* is accessible. In other words, if *x* is accessible, then each descending chain ending with *x_0* is finite. Well-foundedness of *<* is shown by proving that every element in *X* is accessible (for some examples, see the definitions of well-foundedness in Rocq and Agda, or [*"POPLMark Reloaded: Mechanizing Proofs by Logical Relations"*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796819000170)). In our setting, we take *X* as the set of all processes and *<* is defined by: *P < Q* iff there exists a forward transition *t: P --[θ]--> Q*. To prove WF, we show that each process is accessible.

The proof of [PCI](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/pci.bel#L3-L14) is immediate, given the symmetry of independence.

We additionally provide a proof of [ID](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/id.bel#L20-L68), following the argument sketched in [*"An Axiomatic Theory for Reversible Computation"*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3648474).

##### Definition 6.2: Event

We encode the "simplified" definition of [event](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/events.bel#L3-L21) as in Definition 6.2. We then prove that it is [equivalent](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/events.bel#L24-L108) to the "general" definition of event in Definition 2.3: given transitions *t*, *u*, *u'* and *t'* as in Definition 6.2, we show that the independence of *t* and *u* propagates to all pairs of adjacent transitions, and that the given commuting square is non-degenerate.

##### Proposition 6.3: Other properties of the LTSI of CCSK<sup>P</sup>

The proofs of [IRE](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/ire.bel#L3-L12), [CIRE](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/cire.bel#L3-L11) and [RPI](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/rpi.bel#L3-L10) are immediate. #### 6.2: Mechanised Proofs of Axioms for CCSK

The [`defs-and-properties.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel) file contains additional definitions and properties needed to state and prove the axioms, and the [`events.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccskp/axioms/events.bel) file contains the simplified definition of events.

The proofs of the axioms and properties for CCSK are mostly carried out by leveraging the bijection to transfer the proofs of the same properties from CCSK<sup>P</sup>; the [`bijection/lemmas-lifting.bel`](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/lemmas-lifting.bel) file contains proofs of properties that can be transferred from one LTS to another. Among them, one difficulty was proving that if two CCSK transitions are equivalent, then their enriched versions in CCSK<sup>P</sup> are also equivalent (second part of Proposition 6.7 below).

##### Proposition 6.7: Events are stable under bijection

The [first part of Proposition 6.7](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/lemmas-lifting.bel#L113-L137) is carried out by induction on the derivation of the equivalence hypothesis. In the base case of the equivalence definition, we rely on the fact that [the forgetful mapping preserves equality of labels](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/lemmas-lifting.bel#L79-L102).

The [second part of Proposition 6.7](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/bijection/lemmas-lifting.bel#L146-L217) is again proved by induction on the derivation of the equivalence hypothesis; however, one cannot proceed as in the first part of the proposition, since the enrichment mapping does not preserve equality of labels. The result would therefore have to be proved by analyzing the structure of the transitions case by case; this approach is computationally infeasible, as the theorem statement involves four CCSK transitions and four CCSK<sup>P</sup> transitions, requiring further case distinctions between forward/backward and open/closed. Instead, by applying the forgetful and enrichment mappings and the proved axioms for CCSK<sup>P</sup>, we identified and established an [auxiliary lemma](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/defs-and-properties.bel#L250-L1990) that provides a sufficient condition to conclude the proof. The auxiliary lemma itself is proved by case analysis on four CCSK transitions and accounts for approximately 1.7k lines of code. More details can be found in the paper and in the inline comments of the corresponding Beluga files.

##### Proposition 6.8: The LTSI of CCSK is pre-reversible; Theorem 6.9: The axiomatic theory holds for the LTSI of CCSK

The proofs of [SP](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/sp.bel#L9-L30), [BTI](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/bti.bel#L3-L33), [WF](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/wf.bel#L3-L24), [PCI](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/pci.bel#L3-L34), [ID](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/id.bel#L20-L67), [IRE](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/ire.bel#L3-L14), [CIRE](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/cire.bel#L3-L16), [RPI](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/rpi.bel#L3-L13) and [ED](https://github.com/CinRC/Formalising-Concurrency-Causality-and-Conflict-via-Independence-in-Reversible-Calculi/blob/cf48a53/code/ccsk/axioms/ed.bel#L3-L21) are mostly straightforward and are carried out by leveraging the bijection to transfer the proofs of the same properties from CCSK<sup>P</sup>.
